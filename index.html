Storytelling

For the leadership update, not the working session

Drawn from the Sep 3, 2026 refresh · AG Case Management

Premium and HVP are supposed to be the same case-management operation running at two different scales. The data says otherwise. Premium closes its win-back cases at a 94% rate (30 of 32); on the same measure, HVP closes barely a quarter of its cases (1 of 4). That's not a rounding difference — it's a different operation wearing the same name.

HVP's open backlog escalates at 25.7%, versus 11.1% for Premium — even though HVP's research queue, once running at nearly four times what its size alone would predict, has now settled in line with volume.

That last part matters, because it means the gap isn't simply "HVP has more cases." Case mix and investigation load have started to normalize between the two pulls we've taken. What hasn't moved is escalation — HVP cases are still going sideways at more than double Premium's rate, and that's the number worth watching before the next business review.

The manager behind the number

Dig one level into HVP's backlog and the escalation story gets a face: David owns 52.7% of every open HVP case in the system. Not a large share. More than half. No manager on the Premium side — not even Tameko, Premium's most-loaded at 25.0% — comes within twenty points of that concentration. Either this is a genuine, unsustainable workload imbalance sitting on one person's desk, or it's an unassigned-case queue that happens to be tagged with David's manager ID. Both explanations are plausible. Neither has been confirmed yet.

The people behind the aging list

Zoom out from the segment-level numbers and two names sit at the top of the aging-case list: Brandi Evans-King and Jay Holland, each carrying two cases that have now gone more than 48 hours without an update. Small numbers, but the kind that compound — a case untouched for two days rarely gets faster to resolve on day three.

What we're watching next

Three threads carry into the next refresh: whether HVP's escalation rate keeps closing the gap with Premium now that its research queue has normalized, whether David's concentration is confirmed as real workload or reassigned away, and whether Brandi's and Jay's aging cases get resolved before a third name joins them. All three are worth carrying into the next check-in.
